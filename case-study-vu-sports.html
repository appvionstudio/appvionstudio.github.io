Case study · Events

Sign-ups that close themselves.

Campus sport runs on a spreadsheet, a group chat, and one person who becomes the bottleneck. This replaced all three.

In short

VU Sports Society is an Android event and role management app for campus sport, built with Jetpack Compose, Firestore and an MVVM architecture with real-time updates and FCM reminders.

Three roles share it: administrators create events, managers run them, and participants register. Filters stack rather than replacing one another, entries lock automatically when a slot limit is reached, and a confirmed entry lives in the app and works offline.

Measured result: event publish flow time reduced 35 percent, with ANRs minimised through correct threading.

How it works, screen by screen.

Why a spreadsheet stops working.

Trials are announced in a group chat. Sign-ups arrive as replies. Somebody copies them into a sheet. The sheet fills past the slot limit and the organiser starts telling people they are on a waitlist that exists only in their head. On the day, nobody is sure who is actually registered.

Every part of that is a coordination problem, not a sports problem, and it lands on one volunteer.

Decisions that shaped it.

Entries lock themselves at the limit

The slot ceiling is enforced when the entry is written, not checked by the organiser afterwards. That single decision removes the waitlist, the awkward message, and the argument about who registered first.

Filters combine instead of replacing each other

Sport, timeframe and availability stack. It sounds trivial and it is the difference between a filter people use and a filter people abandon after the second tap resets the first.

Real-time lists without the ANRs

Firestore listeners with composite indexes behind them, and work kept off the main thread. Real-time updates are the easiest way to build a janky Android app, and correct threading is the difference between live and frozen.

Reminders that arrive before the event, not after

FCM reminders are scheduled against the event, so a participant who registered three weeks ago is told the day before. Attendance at campus events is mostly a memory problem.

What it changes for an organiser.

  • Publishing an event got 35 percent faster, measured across the flow from creation to live.
  • The waitlist disappears. Capacity is enforced by the system, so no one has to police it.
  • Registration is verifiable at the gate. A confirmed entry lives in the app and works without a signal.
  • The volunteer stops being the bottleneck. Roles mean managers run their own events without going through one administrator.
