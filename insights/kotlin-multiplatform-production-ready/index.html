Is Kotlin Multiplatform production-ready in 2026?

By Ayyaz Sajjad, Founder & CEO · Published 26 Oct 2026

Yes. JetBrains declared Kotlin Multiplatform stable in November 2023, Google has officially supported it for sharing business logic between Android and iOS since May 2024, and Compose Multiplatform for iOS reached stable in May 2025. Netflix, McDonald’s, Cash App and Google Docs run it in production. The useful question now is whether it fits your app.

In production

Who uses Kotlin Multiplatform in production?

JetBrains keeps a public list of Kotlin Multiplatform case studies, each linking to the company’s own write-up. Six of them, and what each one shares:

Netflix

Business logic for its Android and iOS studio apps, used in TV and film production. The team described the approach on the Netflix TechBlog in October 2020.

McDonald’s

Started with the payments feature of the McDonald’s app, then moved the whole app to Kotlin Multiplatform, in a case study published with the agency Umain.

Cash App

Shares its business logic across platforms with Kotlin Multiplatform, a move that started in 2018 when it replaced shared JavaScript.

Google Docs

Uses Kotlin Multiplatform for shared business logic in the Docs app on Android, iOS and the web, according to Google’s 2024 announcement.

Forbes

Shares more than 80 percent of the logic in its mobile app.

Autodesk

Shares offline sync logic and data models across iOS, Android and Windows.

Netflix

Does Netflix use Kotlin Multiplatform?

Yes, for its studio apps: the Android and iOS tools used during TV and film production. Netflix’s engineers wrote the business logic once in Kotlin and compiled it for both platforms, and described the approach on the Netflix TechBlog in October 2020. JetBrains lists Netflix among its Kotlin Multiplatform case studies.

Looking ahead

Is Kotlin Multiplatform the future?

No one can promise where a framework will be in five years, but the signals point one way. Google supports Kotlin Multiplatform officially and keeps adding multiplatform support to Jetpack libraries, JetBrains made Compose for iOS stable in 2025, and the public list of production users keeps growing. For shared business logic it is now a mainstream choice, not an experiment.

Is it worth it?

Is Kotlin Multiplatform worth it for your app?

Production-ready is not the same as right for every product. The deciding question is where your app’s value sits.

Worth it when

Your app needs Android and iOS, its value sits in business rules, offline data or payments, and a wrong rule on one platform would cost money. Those rules are written, tested and fixed once. It is also the natural next step for an existing Kotlin Android app that now needs iOS.

Not worth it when

You only need one platform and have no near-term plan for the second, or the product is mostly screens with very little logic, so there is little worth sharing. Then a single native app, or Flutter for one shared interface, is the simpler choice. See KMP vs Flutter and KMP vs React Native.

FAQ

Questions people ask first.

Is Kotlin Multiplatform free?

Yes. Kotlin Multiplatform is open source and built by JetBrains, with no licence fee. The cost of a KMP app is the engineering time plus the usual store accounts in your own name: the Apple Developer Program at $99 a year and Google Play Console at a one-off $25.

Can Kotlin Multiplatform share the interface as well?

Yes, if you want it to. Compose Multiplatform shares screens across Android and iOS and has been stable on iOS since May 2025. Sharing the interface is optional: many teams share only the logic and keep SwiftUI on iOS where a fully native feel matters most.

Do we have to rewrite our Android app to use it?

No. A well-layered Kotlin Android app can move its business rules and data layer into a shared module one feature at a time while the Android app keeps shipping. How a migration runs.
