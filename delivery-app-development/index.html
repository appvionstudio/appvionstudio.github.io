The real problem

Courier disputes are won by whoever has evidence.

In D. Delivery the goods were phones, laptops and electronics, where one disputed parcel can cost more than a month of delivery revenue. Bookings ran on WhatsApp, cash was reconciled by hand, and riders were assigned by whoever answered first.

No record of condition

Nobody could prove what state a device was in at pickup.

Cash that never matched

Cash on delivery was reconciled by hand at the end of the day.

Dispatch by phone

The first rider to answer got the job, not the nearest one.

Customers calling for status

There was nothing for them to look at.

What we build

Three apps, one source of truth.

Customer app

Booking with address search, insurance based on declared value, live tracking with a traffic-aware ETA, receipt and rating.

Rider app

Job alerts, guided pickup and drop-off, dual video proof, recipient e-signature, wallet and earnings, offline-first sync.

Admin control tower

Manual dispatch, rider approval and documents, pricing, dispute flags and end-of-day cash settlement against the ledger.

Expanding-radius dispatch

New orders search a small radius first and widen step by step until a rider accepts, with the admin alerted if nobody does.

Evidence pipeline

Videos and signatures are compressed on the phone and stored per order; uploads queue in the background on weak signal.

Money on the record

Cash on delivery is settled against the platform's own ledger, not the rider's word.

FAQ

Questions people ask first.

Do you build the rider app and the admin panel too?

Yes. A delivery business needs three products: a customer booking app, a rider job app and an admin control tower. In D. Delivery all three share one Kotlin codebase and one database.

How does proof of delivery work?

The rider records a video at pickup and at drop-off, and the recipient signs on the phone. The evidence is compressed on the device and stored under the order, so it holds up in a dispute.

Can riders keep working on a weak signal?

Yes. Uploads are queued by a background worker, so a rider is never blocked from finishing a job by the network.
