Case study · Education

Built for a campus where the wifi keeps dropping.

Announcements that only arrive when the network is good are announcements that do not arrive. WUM Connect assumes the connection will fail and is designed around that.

In short

WUM Connect is a role-aware university attendance and communication app for Android, built with Jetpack Compose, MVVM and Firestore, on top of an offline-first sync engine using Room and WorkManager.

Students, teachers and administrators open the same app and see only what applies to their role, enforced by Firestore security rules with role-based access control rather than by hiding screens. Attendance marked without a connection is queued locally and reconciles on reconnect.

Measured engineering results: p95 frame time improved 18 percent, cold start reduced by 320ms, and sync success around 99 percent on low connectivity.

How it works, screen by screen.

The problem with campus software.

A university has four audiences with different rights and one shared reality: the network is unreliable. Notices go out by noticeboard, WhatsApp group and email, so nobody knows who actually received anything. Attendance is taken on paper and typed up later, which means the record is always a day behind and occasionally invented.

Most campus apps fail for the same reason. They are built as if the connection is always there, so a teacher in a concrete lecture theatre taps submit, sees a spinner, and goes back to paper.

Decisions that shaped it.

Offline-first as the default path, not a fallback

Attendance writes go to Room first and are queued through WorkManager with backoff and retry. The queue is idempotent, so a retry after a partial failure cannot double-mark a student. Sync success sits around 99 percent on poor connectivity because the app never depended on the network being there at the moment of the tap.

Roles enforced in Firestore rules, not in the interface

Role-based access control lives in security rules with composite indexes behind the queries. Hiding a screen from a student does not stop a determined student from reading the underlying collection; a rule does.

Performance treated as a feature and measured

Cold start was cut by 320ms and p95 frame time improved 18 percent. On the mid-range Android hardware that dominates a campus, those are the differences between an app people open and an app people avoid.

App Check and Crashlytics from the start

App Check keeps unverified clients out of the backend, and Crashlytics turns a vague report of intermittent closures into a stack trace. Both are cheap on day one and expensive to retrofit.

What it changes for an institution.

  • Delivery becomes measurable. An administrator can see how many people received a notice and how many opened it, which no noticeboard has ever done.
  • Attendance is same-day. Marked at the point of teaching rather than typed up later, so the record reflects the room.
  • Bad connectivity stops causing lost data. Work queued offline lands when the signal returns, without the teacher thinking about it.
  • One app replaces four channels. Notices stop being duplicated across noticeboard, email, and messaging groups where nobody can confirm receipt.
